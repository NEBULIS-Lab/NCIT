# Camera hardware assets

Downloaded 2026-09-07. No camera housing or wrist-holder mesh was hand-modelled for this demo.

## RealSense D435

- Source: [RealSense ROS description](https://github.com/realsenseai/realsense-ros/blob/ros2-master/realsense2_description/meshes/d435.dae).
- License: Apache-2.0, retained in `REALSENSE-LICENSE`.
- Upstream original (not shipped in this optimized website bundle): `d435.dae`, SHA256 `42f3b66f47a1f8f425a2e4dc07c1d9c283183167d8441f520a15623d98f9bf78`.
- Derived: `d435.glb`, generated by `scripts/prepare-assembly-camera-assets.mjs`. CAD converted to indexed GLB, bounded-error simplification (relative error at most 0.0005), silver housing / dark optical face recoloring. Original dimensions approximately 90 × 25 × 25 mm are preserved.
- CAD frame: +Z points out of the front glass, X is the long axis, Y vertical. RGB lens center X = 32.5 mm. The virtual RGB view uses 42.5° vertical FOV and 16:9 aspect. These are nominal simulation parameters, not per-device calibration or a RealSense depth/noise model.

## Panda wrist holder

- Source: [ViSP ROS Panda D435 holder](https://github.com/lagadic/visp_ros/blob/master/tutorial/franka/real-robot/franka-rs-D435-camera-holder.stl).
- License: GPL-2.0, retained in `VISP-LICENSE`; the unmodified STL is supplied here as `panda-d435-mount.stl`.
- SHA256 `f3542fa7b6a4b877a1935b63219a6ec07a28d9d58a6ef994122492c200e4eb4a`.
- Reference: [ViSP real-robot visual servoing tutorial](https://docs.ros.org/en/melodic/api/visp_ros/html/tutorial-franka-real-ibvs-apriltag_8cpp-example.html) includes a measured camera transform for its real setup. We reuse the mount mesh, but do not misrepresent that real setup's calibration as this scene's extrinsics. Here the holder is rotated 180° about the hand flange axis to keep camera hardware outside the two-wrist handover gap.
- [Franka's official D435 mount](https://franka.de/models-and-simulation) was also inspected; its published guide targets FR3, so it was not substituted for the Panda-specific mesh.

## Physics and scope

One shared MuJoCo world drives all five RGB views. Wrist camera mass is 72 g and the holder mass is estimated at 15 g. Collision envelopes are simplified, not optical meshes; contacts remain enabled. Existing robot inertials, controllers and trajectories are not rewritten. Camera-frame poses are rigid to the hand, without dynamically steering the lens to an object or hiding occluders.
